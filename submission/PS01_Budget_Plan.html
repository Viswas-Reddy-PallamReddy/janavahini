3 · The pilot and two full-site options
Pilot · Kotilingala model compartment ₹3.7 – 5.5 lakh

6 counter nodes (3 gates, 3 exits), 5 density nodes (road, junction, 3 compartments) with our own cameras, 1 gateway. Own cameras let us measure camera accuracy against manual counts and the state's CCTV.

Tier A · reuse state CCTV Recommended ₹30 – 50 lakh

The state is already installing CCTV at entry and exit points [11]. Density is computed from that CCTV and sent to the nodes. If the network fails, a wide-area node reports at least WATCH, which was safe in all 40 simulated camera-failure runs but means more holds.

Tier B · our own camera on every wide area ₹46 – 72 lakh

A camera is wired to each of the 108 density nodes, so wide-area density survives a network failure. Tier A plus own cameras only where CCTV does not reach falls between the two; the pilot data decides where.

6 · Go / no-go before full-site spending

Most of the money is spent only after the pilot passes these six checks. Each threshold comes from what the simulations needed.

  1. Counters: within ±5% of manual counts at the pilot gates and exits. The Kalman filter was tested with 2–5% counter bias.
  2. Camera density: a reading at least every 2 minutes with 20% error or less, measured against manual counts. This is the requirement from our sensitivity runs.
  3. Radio: at least 70% of mesh messages delivered. The simulations assumed 30% loss.
  4. Uptime: every silent node reported to staff; node uptime at least 99% over the pilot days.
  5. Response time: in drills, the police at a road head act within 60 s of their light changing. In our field simulation (E14), with 70% of the crowd complying, up to 60 s of delay stayed safe in 20 of 20 surge draws; 2–3 minute delays kept only 14 and 9 of 20 safe.
  6. Police review: warning lead time and the number of false alerts per day accepted by the police in charge of the pilot.

If a check fails, we fix the cause and re-test before buying for the full site; the pilot hardware is not wasted, because it becomes part of the full site.

8 · Support required from the organisers and government stakeholders
  1. Kotilingala model-compartment layout, planned pedestrian routes and ghat capacities, to build the route map.
  2. Access to the CCTV feeds at entry and exit points and to the ICCC/RTGS integration interface. Tier A depends on this.
  3. Permission and police coordination for a live pilot at Maha Shivaratri (Feb–Mar 2027), including mounting points and power at gates and junctions.
  4. Power points at node locations, laid with the event's temporary lighting.
  5. Police and volunteer time for drills on the three-colour protocol.
  6. A server or VM in the RMC or state data centre.
  7. Procurement of 244 nodes and 10 gateways through GeM or the ICCC system integrator.
