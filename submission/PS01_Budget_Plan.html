Janavahini: what it costs and how we get it to the ghats by June 2027

About ₹4,400–7,500 per counter node. A one-compartment pilot costs about ₹0.5–1.1 lakh of hardware; the full site, about 250 nodes, about ₹11–19 lakh. Software is open-source, and every node is reused after the event.

5 · Support required from the organisers and government stakeholders
  1. Kotilingala model-compartment layout, planned pedestrian routes and ghat capacities, to build the route map.
  2. Access to existing CCTV crowd-count feeds and the ICCC/RTGS integration interface.
  3. Permission and police coordination for a live pilot at Maha Shivaratri (Feb–Mar 2027), including mounting points and power at gates and junctions.
  4. Police and volunteer time for drills on the three-colour protocol.
  5. A server or VM in the RMC or state data centre.
  6. Procurement support for about 250 nodes.
